Area of Irregular Shapes

A slow, visual walk through how to find the area of any wonky, stepped, or L-shaped figure — no memorising required, just seeing how it works.

01
Let's start simple

What does "area" actually mean?

Picture icing a rectangular cake, or laying square tiles across a kitchen floor. Area is just: how many little squares fit inside a shape? Instead of counting every single square by hand, we count in fast groups — which is exactly what multiplication is for.

That's the whole idea behind area. Every square in that grid got a number, and if you count them one by one you get 15 — the exact same answer as multiplying the rows by the columns. The formula is just a shortcut for counting.

02
The one formula you actually need

Area of a rectangle

For any plain rectangle (or square), area is:

Area = length × width
Notice we never had to memorise this — it fell straight out of the counting-squares picture above. If you ever forget the formula, just picture the grid again!
03
The big trick of this whole topic

Wonky shapes are just rectangles in disguise

An L-shape, a staircase, a shape with a chimney poking out the top — these all look scary at first. But every single one of them can be cut into two or more plain rectangles. Once it's cut, you already know what to do with each piece.

  1. Cut it. Draw one line across the shape so it becomes two (or more) simple rectangles. There's often more than one correct place to cut!
  2. Measure each piece. Find the length and width of every rectangle. Sometimes a side isn't labelled — more on that soon.
  3. Multiply each piece. Area = length × width, for every rectangle separately.
  4. Add them all up. The total area is the sum of every piece's area. Don't forget your units!
Think of it like cutting an oddly-shaped chocolate bar into neat rectangular chunks, working out how many little squares are in each chunk, then adding it all together. 🍫
04
Worked example — side by side pieces

An L-shaped bedroom

Imagine a bedroom shaped like an L — maybe there's a little nook for a wardrobe. Here are its measurements. Let's find its floor area, one step at a time.

  1. Cut: the dashed line splits the room into a top box and a bottom box.
  2. Measure: Rectangle 1 is 3 m × 4 m. Rectangle 2 is 5 m × 3 m.
  3. Multiply: 3 × 4 = 12 m². 5 × 3 = 15 m².
  4. Add: 12 m² + 15 m² = 27 m².

This exact shape and these exact numbers are the worked example already printed at the top of your worksheet — so if this makes sense, you can flip back to it and you'll recognise every step!

05
The one sneaky part

What if a side isn't labelled?

Sometimes a shape gives you the total length of a side, plus part of it — and expects you to work out the rest yourself. This is just take-away thinking, nothing new.

It's exactly like a chocolate bar that's 9 squares long. If you've already counted 5 of them, you don't need to recount the whole bar — you just know 4 are left. Total minus known part = missing part. This is the skill your worksheet is really testing, hidden inside almost every question.

06
Worked example — a piece stacked on top

Not every cut is side-by-side

Sometimes the second rectangle sits centred on top of the first, like a candle poking out of a birthday cake, instead of sitting next to it. Same four steps still work perfectly.

The exact same four steps: cut, measure, multiply, add. It doesn't matter whether the second rectangle sits beside the first one or on top of it — the method never changes.

07
Quick reference

Your cheat sheet

Rectangle area

Area = length × width

Irregular shape area

Cut into rectangles → find each area → add them all together.

Missing side

Total side − the part you know = the missing part.

Always finish with

Units! cm², m², mm² — area is always "squared" because it's measured in squares.

mm² — tiny, like a fingernail cm² — like a postage stamp m² — like a bedroom floor

Now, back to your worksheet

Mapping the method onto your 4 questions

Question 1

A shape with a step cut out of the bottom-right corner. Look for two side-by-side rectangles, just like the bedroom example.

Question 2

Question 3
