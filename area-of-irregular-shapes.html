The amount of flat space inside a shape — like how much carpet covers a floor.

A rectangle is our reliable building block: length multiplied by width.

Partition means split a bigger shape into smaller parts that are easier to see.

tile it in your mind

The one idea underneath

Area = length × width

We are counting equal rows of square units. Length tells us how many squares are in one row; width tells us how many rows there are.

Imagine laying tiles in a room.

If a rectangular room is 6 metres long and 4 metres wide, each row has 6 square metres of tiles and there are 4 rows.

6 × 4 = 24 m² of floor space. An irregular floor plan is still made of floor space — it just needs more than one rectangle to describe it.

same map, every time

A repeatable strategy

Four small moves.

When a worksheet shape looks unfamiliar, return to these four moves instead of guessing.

1

Spot

Look for the straight edges and collect the measurements. Mark missing lengths by thinking about the whole side.

2

Split

Draw a friendly line through the shape. Aim to make two or more complete rectangles.

3

Solve

For each rectangle, use length × width. Write the unit beside every area.

4

Stitch

Add the rectangle areas. Then check: does the total seem sensible for the size of the whole shape?

Let’s walk through the L-shape.

This is the same kind of shape shown on the exercise sheet. We will think aloud, one piece at a time.

length × width5 × 3 = 15 m²
Stitch the pieces together12 m² + 15 m² = 27 m²
What did we not do?

We did not multiply every visible number together. Each multiplication belonged to one rectangle.

How can we check?

The whole shape is a little bigger than a 5 m × 5 m square, so 27 m² feels reasonable.

The labels matter

Area needs square units.

Units tell us what the answer is measuring. Keep them with your working so the meaning stays visible.

If lengths are...Area is written as...Example
millimetres (mm)square millimetres (mm²)48 mm²
centimetres (cm)square centimetres (cm²)62 cm²
metres (m)square metres (m²)27 m²

Quick check

If one side says cm and another says m, pause before calculating. Convert them to the same unit first.

“Same unit in, square unit out.”
pencil time

Now, have a go

Use the map, not memory.

Try each one on paper first. Then open the gentle hint to compare your thinking.

A complete solution should show the split, the small rectangle areas, the addition, and the square unit.

1. A floor is split into rectangles of 6 m × 5 m and 4 m × 2 m. What is the total area?

Try: 6 × 5 = 30 m², then 4 × 2 = 8 m². Stitch them: 30 + 8 = 38 m².

2. A 12 mm × 6 mm rectangle has a 4 mm × 3 mm notch taken away. What remains?

Try: Find the whole first: 12 × 6 = 72 mm². Find the missing notch: 4 × 3 = 12 mm². Take it away: 72 − 12 = 60 mm².

3. Draw your own irregular shape using two rectangles. Give it friendly measurements and find its area.

Draw: Make an L-shape. Label every side you know. If a side is missing, use the whole side to work it out. Then choose a split and explain why it works.
